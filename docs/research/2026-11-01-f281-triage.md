# F281 triage: the wider class of broken corpus documents

> **Status: Living (2026-11-01).** Read-only triage. Nothing was changed in any database, function or data file,
> and no paid API was called. It proposes fixes; it does not authorise any of them (overwriting an existing source
> needs its own authorisation, `docs/operations/corpus-jobs.md`). It extends
> `docs/research/2026-11-01-f281-broken-documents.md` (called "the first report" below); read that for the replace
> route and the fix mechanics, which are not repeated here.
>
> **Evidence labels.** "Executed" means a command was run and its output observed. "Inferred" means a conclusion
> drawn from executed results, not itself observed. Stored text always comes from the local replica
> (`niyantran_retrieval_replica`, read-only). Identifiers are 8-character document id prefixes.

## 1. Summary

**Class 1, Bills that may hold another bill's text.** The first report flagged 261 Bill documents whose title stem is
missing from their first two chunks, and said 104 of them share no significant word with the text. The list of the 104
was not saved, and a simple word-overlap test I wrote did not reproduce the number (it gave 82 to 88 depending on
the word rules). So I triaged the whole 261, plus 3 more that my own test flagged: **264 documents**, which contain
the 104 by construction.

| Verdict | Count | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Genuine mismatch | 43 | The stored text is another bill, act or gazette. In all 43 the PDF at `file_url` is the same wrong file (39 fetched here, page 1 read by OCR, 82 to 100 percent of its distinctive words are in the stored opening; the other 4 were fetched for the first report). So the defect is at sansad, never in our import |
| Garbage OCR | 9 | Hallucinated or unreadable OCR in all (3) or part (6) of the document; a different defect, found on the way |

| Unclear | 1 | `cc99b0d2` |

**Class 3.** `40b1cec9`: confirmed, the first 237 characters are the cover of another bill. `410ebf75`: confirmed, three
stray glyphs in one chunk (not a shifted chunk). Both detailed in section 5.

**Class 4, shifted-font scan.** Across all 2,896 Bills and 474 Regulatory documents, control characters appear in
exactly four documents: the two known (`fb64d5ac`, `1f1ce52a`), `410ebf75` (2 characters), and a new, trivial hit
`9726bbf4` (10 characters in the last chunk). No Regulatory hit. Section 6.

**New findings beyond the brief** (section 8): the stray-cover problem is a whole Rajya Sabha 2000 series (14
documents, not 2); sansad's synopsis and gazette files for Rajya Sabha bills are filed under the Lok Sabha number
(so 9 synopsis documents hold the wrong act, and 3 gazette documents too), and the Class 2 truncation has a regular shape (the stored text starts at
page 7, 21, 27 or 33), which points at the ingestion job, not at the scans.

## 2. Class 1: method

- **Executed.** Read-only SQL on the replica for all 2,896 Bill documents (opening, key, role, page count, full
  text for the 264). For each of the 264: the title's 5-letter stems were counted in the whole document, not only the
  first two chunks, and the bill title and "Bill No." printed in the stored opening were extracted by pattern and
  compared with the stated key and title.
- **Executed, the fetch.** 47 PDFs from sansad `getFile` (44 for the mismatch candidates plus three singles) were
  downloaded and page 1 read by local OCR (a small Swift tool of my own over Apple Vision, run on the Mac; no
  network, nothing paid; note that it does not read Devanagari). The sansad JSON listing
  (`api_rs/legislation/getBills`, filtered by `billYear` and `billNumber`) was queried for each text-bill, to see
  which file sansad itself lists for it.
- **Inferred.** The mechanism in section 3; the amendment-renumbering explanations in the false-alarm table; that
  the stated bill's own text is not at any other sansad URL (each record has one file field, and I did not look at
  other hosts).
- Not done: spot-checking the 108 key-less documents against sansad by file-name number (they have no stated
  title to contradict); desk-record checks (no `desk_rows` in the replica, the supervisor reads NTER).

## 3. Class 1: the genuine mismatches (43)

**The mechanism (executed for the Lok Sabha group, inferred for the rest).** For 24 of 24 Lok Sabha mismatches the
sansad record of the text-bill (found by the number and year printed on the PDF) links **the same file** as the
stated bill (one differs only in letter case, `363ls.pdf` against `363LS.pdf`). So sansad lists one `NNNNLS.pdf` for
two bills, and the corpus built its title and key from the wrong record. In every case the bill that really owns the
file, under its own number, has no document of that role in the corpus. The Rajya Sabha and synopsis cases follow a second
pattern (inferred from the file names): sansad names synopsis and gazette files `YYYY-N-...` by the Arabic number, so a Rajya Sabha bill
"LXXI" and a Lok Sabha bill "71" of the same year share one name. The bill keys that already exist are named in
the Fix column.

## Addendum: where the Regulatory truncation comes from (supervisor, replica read-only, 2026-11-01)

For every indexed Regulatory document of 5 or more pages (202), the stored text length equals the pack's own
`metadata.n_chars` (within 5 characters): 45 of 45 thin documents and 157 of 157 others. All carry `ocr_lang`
`eng`. So the truncated text arrived that way in the client's pack: our import stored what the pack held. The cut
is in the pack's OCR, not in our ingestion code.

Consequences:
- Repairs re-OCR from the official PDFs; re-importing the pack text would restore the truncation.
- The bulk pipeline (F278) ingests from the same packs. Its acceptance checks must test text coverage per page
  (characters per page against `n_pages`, and a sample against the official file) and route scanned documents
  with thin text to OCR from the source, rather than trusting pack text.
- The 157 documents above the 600-characters-a-page line were not compared with their official files, so partial
  truncation among them is not ruled out. A sample check belongs in the repair batch.
